## Assumptions and scope

- Fake Odoo uses a synthetic data model. Its services and product configurations are demo extensions. The customer version and schema require separate verification.
- The demo has one workspace. Both roles can read its deliveries. Admin manages crews, participants, skills, and templates. Operator handles operational actions.
- New planning requires an address, city, and phone. The elevator field distinguishes unknown from no.
- Indoor/outdoor and area values serve as literal classifications and filters. Route optimization is outside this stage.
- Issue and photo types use fixed prototype enums, and task templates are configurable.
- A JSONB aggregate and a global transaction advisory lock support the small stack. Scans are limited to 5,000 records.
- At this stage, result submissions used only the explicitly local CLI or gateway through shared domain rules with no public author impersonation endpoint. Crew and customer authentication and product UI were outside this stage.
- Signatures are synthetic and labelled DEMO. Customer electronic signatures are outside this stage.
- This stage covered a local demo. Real Odoo or customer connections, commercial orders, writes to Odoo, front office, payments, routing, brokers, microservices, and deployment were outside its scope.
- Files are validated by name, size, encoding, and real image or PDF decoders. Responses retain attachment and nosniff headers. Antivirus and production document sandboxing are separate controls.
- SQL, JSON, HTTP, and configuration enter as `unknown` and are validated at runtime. The implementation avoids authored `any`, unsafe double assertions, and suppression of checks.
